## 1. Analysis

**Goal:** say, with cases and counts, **where the pose of the pipeline is wrong on real footage, how wrong, and whether the model's own score warns about it**, in the four situations of the plan (a hand over the trunk, the trunk turning, standing on one leg, the back to the camera) and in anything else the review finds. The report is the input of three decisions: the **mask rule of P4.01** (`tau_kp = 0.3` and the essential joints are only proposals in W6), the **left/right swap detector of P4.06**, and the **supported views and the camera positions** of the capture protocol (P1.05, P1.09) and of the metric records.

The point that makes this more than a list of pictures: **a score mask catches only the errors the model knows about.** The dangerous failures are the confident ones (a left/right swap, a hidden limb put on a plausible place) with a score above the threshold. So for every wrong joint the review records the score the model gave it, and the report states the share of failures that a mask at `tau_kp` would **not** remove.

Plan row P3.08: *"Review the pose errors on real clips: hand over the trunk, trunk rotation, one-leg stand, back to the camera; write the failure cases"*, deliverable `reports/p3_failure_cases.md`, test "a list with images". Plan risk table: *"Pose fails often when the trunk turns or a hand covers it -> limit the supported views, adjust the camera position, write the limit into the metric record."*

**In scope:**
- `taiji_ai/src/taiji_ai/audit/` (new package, logic with unit tests): `signals.py` (per-frame measures of the target's pose: score signals, structure, speed, bone length, left/right swap, and four finders for the four situations), `select.py` (which frames to look at: rank-based, spaced in time, one blind random sample, overlaps merged), `render.py` (the overlay and the contact sheets), `labels.py` (the review file: vocabulary, validation, the score of each labelled joint), `summary.py` (the tables of the report).
- `tools/pose_audit.py` (CLI: `scan` runs the pose over the videos in chunks, computes the signals, selects the frames and writes the sheets; `report` reads the review file and writes the tables).

- The report `Docs/reports/p3_failure_cases.md` (counts, tables, the list of cases by ID, video label, time and frame index; **no image, no frame, no keypoint file in git**), README, plan rows, tracking, DEC.

- P3.01 and P3.05 already showed D20 frames: frontal (120, 300, 480 s of video 2), profile lunge (660, 840 s), up to four people (660 s). It is a lecture: the instructor demonstrates the 24 forms, so it holds turns, one-leg stands and backs, but also talking and slow breakdowns, and its geometry is not our capture protocol.
- In the dry run of P3.07 the **feet were the group with the largest keypoint difference** between N = 5 and N = 1 and the arms next: the likely hot spots of this review.
- `check_joint_order` is made for an upright person and says `undecidable` otherwise, so on a lunge or a turn it cannot be the judge; here it is only one of the signals.
- The ontology already names the vocabulary the review should use for the view (`front, side, oblique, back, unknown`) and for the situation (`occlusion`, `orientation_change`); "one leg" is the forms `lower_stand_one_leg`, kicks and similar.
- X.17: there is no coach for now. Judging whether a keypoint sits on the right joint does not need Tai Chi expertise; naming the form does, and the review does not need it (time and video label are enough to find a frame).

**What has to be decided (the reasoning, worked out while reading):**

1. **Which frames.** A random sample of a lecture rarely shows a back view or a one-leg stand, and looking at thousands of frames is not possible. So the frames come from (a) **signals** that point at suspect poses, (b) **finders** that point at the four situations, and (c) a **blind random sample** of the frames no signal chose, so that the report can say how often a failure goes unflagged. Everything is **rank-based, never a fixed threshold** (AGENTS.md 5.5): "the 20 worst by this measure, at least 2 s apart".
2. **The signals and finders (all on the target track of P3.06, the person P4 will use):**

   | Name | Measure | What it points at |
   | ---- | ------- | ----------------- |
   | `low_essential` | number of essential joints with score under `tau_kp` (0.3) in the frame | frames the mask of P4.01 would blank, and their neighbours |
   | `structure` | `check_joint_order` fails on an upright frame (side rule, centre points, feet near ankles) | implausible poses, swaps |
   | `jump` | per joint, displacement per frame time in body heights per second | flicker, swaps, hidden limbs jumping |
   | `bone` | 2D length of each bone against the clip median of that bone | impossible limbs |
   | `swap_pair` | a left/right pair whose labels jump to each other's place at once (the cost of crossing minus the cost of staying) | left/right swaps (a preview of P4.06; tested on SYN-08) |
   | `target_event` | `lost`, `target_lost`, `reattached`, `needs_reselect` of P3.06 | person and target errors |
   | `find_back` | mean score of the face joints low while the body joints are high | back to the camera |
   | `find_one_leg` | height difference of the ankles over the leg length | one foot raised |
   | `find_rotation` | apparent shoulder width over torso length, and how fast it changes | profile and turning |
   | `find_occlusion` | distance of the nearer wrist to the middle of the trunk over the trunk width | a hand in front of the trunk |
   | `blind` | uniform random, seeded, among tracked frames no other rule chose | what the signals miss, and the base rate |

   The finders are **sampling aids**: they bring candidates of a situation; the reviewer says what the situation really is, and the report gives how often the finder was right.
3. **The review budget.** 160 frames: 20 per signal or finder for the ten rules above (200 less overlaps, kept at about 120 after merging) and 40 blind, shown 12 to a sheet (about 14 sheets). Frames are spread over the four videos in proportion to their length. A `jump` candidate is shown as a strip of three consecutive frames, because flicker cannot be seen in one.
4. **The review and who does it.** Each frame has a stable ID (`fc-001`, ...), the label of the video, the time and the frame index. For each frame the reviewer records: `verdict` (`ok` or `failure`), the `failure_type`, the **joints** that are wrong (names or groups), the `severity`, the `view` (`front, side, oblique, back, unknown`) and the `situation` (`none, occlusion, orientation_change, one_leg`). **A fills the file first from what A sees on the sheets; H then confirms or corrects** and the report gives the agreement per field. The score of each named joint comes **from the data**, not from the reviewer, so that "the score did not warn" is a measured fact.
5. **The taxonomy and the severity, fixed before looking:**

   | Failure type | Meaning |
   | ------------ | ------- |
   | `lr_swap` | left and right labels of a limb or of the whole body are on the wrong sides |
   | `joint_off` | a joint is visibly off its true place (more than about a hand width) |
   | `hidden_guess` | a joint that is hidden (behind the body or another limb) is placed on a plausible spot that cannot be checked |
   | `foot_error` | toes or heels wrong (the feet are the hard group) |
   | `flicker` | a joint jumps between neighbouring frames (strip of three) |
   | `wrong_person` | the keypoints are on someone or something else |
   | `missed_person` | the person is in the picture and no skeleton is returned |
   | `other` | with a written note |

   Severity: **S1** a limb on the wrong side or on another body part, or no person; **S2** a joint visibly off; **S3** minor (a few pixels, jitter that does not change the pose). S3 is not counted as a failure in the rates, only listed.
6. **Which N.** The pass runs the **shipping pipeline**: the N that the report of P3.07 recommends (5 unless the rule says otherwise). Every flagged window is **rerun at N = 1** (cheap: seconds) and the failure is marked `cadence` if it disappears there, so that model failures are not mixed with failures of the box-from-keypoints shortcut.
7. **Scale and chunks.** `run_pose` is made for a segment of a few minutes; the four videos are run in chunks of 300 s (plus the 1 s margins of `run_pose`, which are dropped when the chunks are joined, frames identified by `frame_index`). One GPU job at a time (AGENTS.md 5.7); the scan runs in a worker process.
8. **What the report may say.** Counts per failure type, view, situation and joint group; the **precision of each signal** (share of its frames that are failures); the **blind rate** with a Wilson 95 % interval (the estimate of how often the pose fails on this footage); the **share of failures the score does not warn about**; the list of every failure case; what it means for P4 and for the capture. It does **not** give an accuracy, a threshold or a claim about a person's movement.

**Constraints that apply:** AGENTS.md 5.1 (nothing is filled: the signals use NaN-aware measures and say undecidable), 5.2 (frames by real PTS: through `run_pose`), 5.3 (raw layer untouched), 5.5 (no universal thresholds: rank-based selection; `tau_kp = 0.3` is W6's proposal and is only **evaluated**, not set), 5.6 (models only from the registry), 5.7 (one GPU job; heavy work in the worker), 5.9 and 9.4 (D20 stays out of git; **no frame, no crop, no sheet, no keypoint file in the repository**; the report names videos `d20-v1..v4` and frames by time and index, never by file name or path), 9.4 point 7 (no dataset content in tests: tests use the synthetic skeleton), 6 (type hints, no `print` in `src`, IDs in logs), 5.12 (a failure type is not a domain rubric; no coach text is invented).

**Unknowns, risks:**

1. *Lecture footage is not our capture:* the instructor is large and well lit, the camera is steady, but the angle changes and there are cuts. The report says so; the failure rates are a first look, to be repeated on the P1.09 clips (follow-up X.28, blocked by P1.09).
2. *The finders may be poor:* a heuristic for "back to the camera" or "one foot raised" can bring many wrong candidates. That is measured (precision per finder) and is itself a result; if a situation is not found, the report says it was not found, not that it does not fail.
3. *Human load:* 14 sheets of 12 frames is about 40 minutes if A has pre-filled the file; without that, twice as long. If H is not available the review stays with A alone and the report says "reviewed by the agent only"; the numbers are then provisional.
4. *Eye judgement of a joint:* "visibly off" is judged on a 1080p crop; occluded joints cannot be judged (`hidden_guess`). The severity scale is coarse on purpose.
5. *Privacy of the images:* they show a person from a web video; they stay in `data/reports/p3_08/` and are shown only to the maintainer (and viewed by the agent, as in P3.01).
6. *Compute:* about an hour of GPU, then CPU work on about 150 000 frames (vectorised); the pose files are about 100 MB and local.
7. *The cadence N* decided by P3.07 may be 1, which doubles the pass.

## 2. Plan

**Design:**

`audit/signals.py` (numpy only, no I/O; all functions take the target track of P3.06 and the clip's times):

- `body_height(keypoints, valid)`: hull height of the valid keypoints of each frame (NaN if undecidable); the unit of the distances.
- `low_essential(scores, tau)`: count of essential joints under `tau` per frame (NaN rows count as all essential joints missing).
- `jump_speed(keypoints, t_sec)`: per frame the largest per-joint speed in body heights per second, using the real `t_sec` (never the frame count), NaN where either frame is missing.
- `bone_deviation(keypoints, bones)`: largest relative deviation of a bone length from the clip median of that bone.
- `swap_margin(keypoints, pairs)`: per frame and pair, `cost_stay - cost_cross` (positive: the two labels are nearer to each other's earlier place), the largest over the pairs.
- finders: `face_vs_body_score`, `ankle_height_difference`, `shoulder_width_ratio` and its rate of change, `wrist_to_trunk_distance`.
- Every function returns a float array of the length of the frames, NaN where the measure cannot be decided; nothing is filled.

`audit/select.py`:

- `Rule(name, score: np.ndarray, higher_is_worse: bool)`, `select_frames(rules, t_sec, video_of_frame, k=20, min_gap_s=2.0, blind_k=40, seed=20261116, weights_by_video)` -> `Candidate(id, video, frame_index, t_sec, tags, scores)`; rank-based per rule, spaced in time, a frame chosen by several rules gets all the tags and counts once, the next-ranked frame takes the free slot; the blind sample is uniform among tracked frames not chosen and not within `min_gap_s` of a chosen one; deterministic for a seed.

`audit/render.py`: `draw_person(image, bbox, keypoints, scores, names)`, `contact_sheet(tiles, columns=4)`, a three-frame strip; NaN keypoints are not drawn; the legend names the video label, the time and the rule tags only.

`audit/labels.py`: `ReviewRow` (id, `a_*` fields filled by A, `h_*` fields filled by H, `joints`, `severity`, `view`, `situation`, `cadence`, `note`), `read_review(path)` strict (vocabulary of point 5 and the ontology, joint names or groups only, severity only with `failure`, every candidate present once), `joint_scores(row, track)` (the score of each named joint from the data), `final_label(row)` (H if given, else A), `agreement(rows)` per field.

`audit/summary.py`: counts per failure type, view, situation and joint group; precision per rule; blind rate with a Wilson 95 % interval; the share of failures with at least one wrong joint whose score is at or above `tau_kp` ("the mask would not remove it"); the list of cases; the markdown tables (`render_tables`, so no number is typed by hand).

`tools/pose_audit.py`: `scan --video-dir ... --out data/reports/p3_08 [--n N]` (throwaway ingest as in P3.07, chunked `run_pose` in a worker process, signals, selection, sheets, `candidates.json`, an empty `review.csv` with the IDs and the data columns, and the second pass at N = 1 on the flagged windows) and `report --out ...` (reads `review.csv`, writes `report_tables.md`); labels only in output.

Refinements:

1. **The cadence is inverted.** The rule of P3.07 recommends **N = 1**, so the pass runs at N = 1 (the reference; it also removes the cadence from the questions about the model) and the **flagged windows are rerun at N = 5**, the default of the code, to mark the failures that depend on the cadence. The cost of the full pass is about 2 hours of GPU (5 912 s of video at RTF 1.23).
2. **The recordings must stay for the whole task**: the sheets and the reruns decode frames from the ingested copies, so the throwaway folder (`data/bench/p3_08/`, ignored by git) is kept until the close and removed there; the pose files of the chunks are written one by one (`data/reports/p3_08/pose/`), so an interrupted pass resumes.
3. **The pose pass starts first.** It needs only `run_pose`, so it is written and started before the signals; it runs in the background while the analysis code and its tests (CPU only) are written. The gate, which has GPU tests, is not run during the pass (one GPU job at a time).
4. **Chunks are analysed one by one**: the target is chosen and followed per chunk of 300 s (default rule at the chunk start; a chunk without a person in its first 2 s has no target and its signals are NaN, recorded as such); the 1 s margins of `run_pose` are dropped when the per-frame signals are joined, frames identified by `frame_index`.
5. **`_ingest` of P3.07 becomes public** (`ingest_throwaway`) because two packages use it.
6. **Persons other than the target** are looked at only when a rule says so (`target_event`, `wrong_person`, `missed_person`): the P3.07 measurement showed that counting every person is dominated by small people far behind the performer.

7. **Added while writing the analysis code (2026-11-16): a second pass at N = 5 and an eleventh rule.** With the primary pass at N = 1, the candidate frames come from N = 1 only, so a failure that **N = 5 introduces** (a limb cut by a box taken from the previous keypoints: the 338 px difference of P3.07 is of this kind) would never be a candidate; and "rerun the flagged windows" answers only half of the question. So the whole video is also run at **N = 5** (about 35 minutes of GPU after the N = 1 pass; same chunks, so the frames align) and a rule `cadence_diff` (the largest distance, in body heights, between the target's keypoints at N = 5 and at N = 1 in a frame) takes 20 more frames; the budget is 180 frames, about 15 sheets. Each tile shows both skeletons (N = 1 and N = 5), and the label `cadence` becomes `both` (wrong at both), `n5_only` (the cadence causes it), `n1_only` (only N = 1 is wrong) or `unknown`, which answers directly which cadence is wrong where.

The answers to Q1 to Q12 are the defaults of the table in section 1, except Q6, which follows refinements 1 and 7 (approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-16).

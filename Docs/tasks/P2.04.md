## 1. Analysis

**Goal:** find how far camera B's timeline is from camera A's at the start and at the end of a take, from the two claps of
each sync signal (capture protocol §5), with ±1 ms precision on clean signals, and say "not found" instead of guessing.

**Facts read (2026-11-15):** W2 steps 1–3 (mono 16 kHz WAV, envelope = absolute value then low-pass, `scipy.signal.correlate`
in a window at the start and at the end, `drift = (offset_end − offset_start) / duration`); T-SYNC-01 (known click offset Δ
found within ±1 ms at 16 kHz), T-SYNC-02 (audio stretched by 0.01 %: drift with the right sign and size), T-SYNC-03 (no click:
"not found", no invented offset). The capture protocol puts ≥ 5 s of recording before the start signal and after the end
signal, two claps about 1 s apart, each with a flash. P2.01 fixes the mapping `t_b = t_a + offset_s + drift × (t_a − t_ref_s)`
on each camera's video timeline.

**Why the A/V start matters:** the audio offset is measured on the audio timelines; the mapping is on the video timelines.
Each file's `audio.start_offset_s` (audio start − video start, from ffprobe, P2.01) converts one to the other. The planning
experiment (synthetic AAC/MP4 from ffmpeg) found audio and video starting together and a click landing within one sample of
its true time; phone files can have encoder priming or edit lists, which P2.05 (flash) and P2.07 (trial) measure.

**In scope:**

1. `io/audio.py`: `extract_audio(recording_dir) -> Path` (`ffmpeg -map 0:a:0 -vn -ac 1 -ar 16000 -c:a pcm_s16le`, atomic,
   cached as `audio.wav`, regenerated if missing), `read_wav(path) -> (samples float32, sr)`.
2. `io/audio_sync.py`: `envelope(x, sr, hp_hz, lp_hz)`, `xcorr_lag(env_a, env_b, sr, max_lag_s) -> LagEstimate`
   (FFT correlation, normalised, parabolic sub-sample peak, `peak`, `peak_ratio`), `estimate_sync(rec_a, rec_b, params) ->
   AudioSync` (start and end windows, offset, drift, `status`), in video time.
3. F-VID fixtures for sync (see the test plan) and tests T-SYNC-01..03 plus robustness cases.

**Out of scope:** flash validation (P2.05), the usability rule and writing `sync.json` and the database row (P2.06),
more than two cameras.

**Design:**

- Envelope: high-pass (removes voice rumble and hum), rectify, low-pass, as W2 says; the cut-offs are engineering parameters
  chosen on the fixtures and written into `params` of `sync.json` (they are not domain thresholds).
- Windows: the start window is the first `w_s` seconds of camera A, matched against the first `w_s + max_lag_s` seconds of B;
  the end window likewise from the end. Defaults `w_s = 30 s`, `max_lag_s = 15 s` (one person presses both phones, so the
  start difference is seconds, not minutes; Q1).
- Found or not: a window is found only when the normalised peak and the peak ratio both pass their minimum; otherwise
  `found = false` and no lag is reported. `status`: `ok` (both), `start_only` (drift `null`), `not_found` (offset `null`).
  No fallback to the phone clock or to zero (AGENTS.md §5.1).
- Drift: `(lag_end − lag_start) / (t_ref_end − t_ref_start)` on camera A's video time; also given in ppm.
- No audio stream in either file: `not_found` with reason `no_audio`.

### 9.2 Analysis (what the probes showed)

- **BUG-0058.** Synthetic takes of 120 s and 300 s (A records `[0, T]`, B starts 0.5 s late, claps 6 s after the later start and 7 s
  before the earlier stop): B stopping 0–30 s after A is found (offset error −0.01 ms, drift 0.1 ppm); B stopping 3 s before A gives
  `start_only`; B stopping 0.4 s before A (or starting early and stopping 1 s before) gives `ok` with the end lag exactly 1 s wrong
  (the other clap of the pair): drift −11111 ppm at 120 s, −3704 ppm at 300 s. Cause: the B end window starts `window + 15 s`
  before the end of B and the lag range is ±15 s from the window starts, so the real offset must lie in
  `[dur_b − dur_a − 30, dur_b − dur_a]`. Every first-version fixture was 10–16 s long (the short-take branch only).
- **BUG-0059.** The offset is measured at the claps but assigned to the centre of the 30 s window (`t_ref_s` = 15 s), and the
  drift divides by the distance of the window centres: 100 ppm is reported as 118.5 ppm at 120 s, 106 ppm at 300 s, offset
  error 0.8 ms; `window_a_s` and `t_ref_s` are on A's audio timeline although the schema says video timeline.
- **Real containers are right** (not a bug): a phone-trimmed clip (edit list) gave −0.700 s exactly; a file whose audio starts
  0.436 s after its video (AAC, `-itsoffset`) gave an offset error of 0.0 ms: the wav starts at the first audio sample, so
  `t_video = t_audio + audio.start_offset_s` holds.
- **BUG-0060, BUG-0061, BUG-0062:** as registered (34 mutants, 14 survived; made-up numbers; 1.15 GB for a 30 min envelope).
- **P2.05 coupling.** `io/flash.py` reads `start.window_a_s`, `end.window_a_s` and `t_ref_s` of the result as camera A's **video**
  timeline (it decodes video over those windows and maps them with `t_b = t_a + offset + drift (t_a − t_ref_s)`), and
  `tests/contract/test_flash.py` calls `estimate_sync(..., settings=...)`. The windows were audio-timeline values: wrong by
  `audio.start_offset_s` of A as soon as it is not 0. This rework makes them what the schema says.

### 9.3 Design (the contract of the rework; the tests are written against this)

**Parameters** (`SyncAudioParams`, recorded in `params` of the result and of `sync.json`): `sr` 16000, `hp_hz` 300, `lp_hz` 50,
`window_s` 30, `max_lag_s` 15 (the search range of the **start** window), `min_peak` 0.25, `min_peak_ratio` 1.25, `min_crest` 2.5, and
new: `max_drift` 2000e-6 (dimensionless, recorded as `max_drift_ppm` 2000.0), `end_margin_s` 3.5, `ratio_floor` 0.01.
*(Correction found while testing the first draft against the T-SYNC-08 case of 9091 ppm: `end_margin_s` was planned as 0.5 s. With a
margin that small, a true match lying just outside the plausible band is not seen, and a **single clap** of the pair (half the peak) is
matched inside the band and accepted as `ok` with a lag that is 1 s wrong. The margin must be wider than the gap of a clap pair
(about 1 s, up to 2 s): the true match of an implausible drift is then the best peak, and the drift limit rejects it; the same guards
against a start window that matched the wrong clap.)*
Single source for the defaults: module constants used by both the dataclass and `xcorr_lag`.

**Start window (as before).** A: first `win_a = min(window_s, 0.45·dur_a if dur_a < 2·window_s else window_s)` seconds; B: first
`min(win_a + max_lag_s, 0.5·dur_b if dur_b < 2·window_s else dur_b − win_a)` seconds. Result `lag0` (audio timelines).

**End window (new).** A: the last `win_a` seconds, `[t0_end_a, dur_a]` with `t0_end_a = max(win_a, dur_a − win_a)`. It is searched
**only if the start was found**, and in B at the place the start offset predicts: the B slice has the same length as A's window and
starts at `t0_end_a + lag0` (samples outside B's recording are zero); the correlation lag range is `±m` with
`m = end_margin_s + max_drift · dt`, `dt = t_ref_end − t_ref_start` (audio time, estimated from the window geometry before the
search); the result is **accepted only if `|drift| <= max_drift`** (the search is wider than the accepted band on purpose, see the
correction above). The end lag is `lag0 + k`. It no longer depends on which phone stopped first. If the start was not found the end is not
searched (status `not_found`, end window `found = false`).

**Matched position (BUG-0059).** `xcorr_lag` also returns `t_match_s`: the centroid, in seconds from the start of A's window, of the
positive part of the product of the two zero-mean envelopes aligned at the best lag (where the matched evidence is). `t_ref_s` is
`t_match` of the start window and `t_ref_end` that of the end window, both converted to A's video timeline with
`+ audio.start_offset_s` of A. `offset_s` is the start lag at `t_ref_s`; `drift = (end_lag − start_lag) / (t_ref_end − t_ref_start)`.
`window_a_s` of both windows is A's **video** timeline: `(max(0, t0 + s_a), t1 + s_a)`. All lags to video time: `+ (s_b − s_a)`.

**Plausibility (BUG-0058).** If `|drift| > max_drift`, or `dt <= 0`, the end window is reported `found = false` (lag `None`, the
measured peak and ratio kept) and the status is `start_only`: a wrong lag can never become `ok`.

**No made-up numbers (BUG-0061).** `peak_ratio = best / max(second, ratio_floor)` (a second peak below the floor counts as the
floor: at most 1 / 0.01 = 100 for a peak of 1, a named parameter, not a sentinel; the ratio is never below 1, the schema
refuses it: a floor above the best peak gives 1.0); when the window is too short to hold a second
peak the match is not unique and `found = false` with `peak_ratio = None`; `peak` is clipped to `[0, 1]` (rounding made an exact
match 1.0000000000000002, which the schema refuses); the "no audio" result carries the windows that would have been searched
(from the container duration), `found = false`, and the drift is never 0 as a fallback.

**API.** `extract_audio(recording_dir)` and `estimate_sync(rec_dir_a, rec_dir_b, *, params=None)` lose the unused `settings`
argument. New exceptions in `io/audio_sync.py`: `SyncError(AudioError)`, `TakeMismatch(SyncError)` (different `take_id`),
`SyncInputError(SyncError)` (unreadable manifest; a wav whose sample rate is not `params.sr`). `read_wav` and `extract_audio`
raise `AudioError` (a timeout has its own message; a failed delete of the temporary file is logged, never swallowed). The envelope
is computed on the windows only (plus 2 s of context), never on the whole recording. `LagEstimate` gains `t_match_s`.

`ratio_floor` 0.01; the exclusion radius of the second peak is **0.2 s** (it was 0.5 s). The **end window** is the last `win_a`
seconds of the stretch both cameras recorded (`min(dur_a, dur_b − lag0)`), not of A alone; B's slice is wider than A's window by the
margin on both sides, cut to what B recorded; `xcorr_lag` takes `center_lag_s`. The start window is searched `max_lag_s` +
`start_guard_s` and a lag beyond `max_lag_s` is refused. `t_match_s` is the centroid of the part of the product above 5 % of its
maximum. New refusals: the same recording or camera twice (`SyncInputError`), audio with dropped samples (`AudioError`, packet
timestamps jumping by more than 2 ms), NaN or inf in an envelope (not found). `read_wav` scales before it averages the channels and
turns any parser error into `AudioError`. Reasons and evidence: DEC-0033.

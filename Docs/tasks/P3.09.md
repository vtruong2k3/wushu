## 1. Analysis

**Goal:** answer one question with numbers, on the same footage and the same machine: **does the detector of the shipping stack (YOLOX-m of rtmlib, weights `yolox_m_humanart`) lose anything that matters against YOLO11?** "Matters" means three things (the plan row): **recall** of the performer, **stability of the performer's box** from frame to frame, **speed** and memory. The result is a **recorded decision** (DEC): either DEC-0005 stands (YOLOX stays; Ultralytics stays out), or there is a gap that justifies looking at a detector with a licence we can ship. Nothing in the stack changes in this task.

**What DEC-0005 already says, and what P3.08 adds.** DEC-0005 (2026-11-13): no Ultralytics YOLO (AGPL-3.0), keep YOLOX, "for one large performer on a fixed camera, detector quality is not the bottleneck", YOLO-pose has no foot keypoints; YOLO11 only as a comparison. P3.08 measured something that bears on that reason: of 250 labelled frames of the four videos the detector returned **no person at N = 1 in one frame** (a dark fade), and in the 15 frames where the target was lost the performer **had been detected** with 25 to 26 valid keypoints (the tracker lost them, not the detector). So the prior is "little to gain"; the comparison is there to check it with a number and not to assume it.

**In scope:**

- A YOLO-format ONNX adapter and the metrics, in `taiji_ai/bench/` (offline tooling; nothing of the product imports `taiji_ai.bench`), with tests.
- An isolated environment **outside the repository and outside `taiji_ai/.venv`** with the Ultralytics package, used **only to export** the weights to ONNX through the `yolo` command line (a separate process; no Python import of Ultralytics anywhere in our code).
- Measurements: speed and memory (fresh process per model), recall on the 250 labelled frames of P3.08, agreement and box stability on the five benchmark clips, a look at the frames where the two detectors disagree; the sensitivity of YOLOX to its own threshold as a by-product.
- The decision rule written **before** any number, the appendix in `Docs/reports/p3_pose_benchmark.md`, DEC, notices, trackers.

**Out of scope:** adopting YOLO11 or any Ultralytics code, registering it in `models/registry.json`, YOLO-pose, other detectors (RTMDet, YOLOX-l and x, RT-DETR: a follow-up only if a gap is found), fp16 or TensorRT, batching, the detector cadence N (decided: 5, DEC-0042), changing the threshold or the NMS of the shipping YOLOX, clips of our own (X.27 and X.28 repeat the measurements when P1.09 exists).

**The shipping detector, as read from the code** (so the comparison is made against what really runs):

- `pose/rtm.py` builds `rtmlib.YOLOX(path, model_input_size=(640, 640), backend="onnxruntime", device="cuda")` and `detect()` returns **boxes only**.
- The ONNX file has **NMS inside the graph**: inputs `input [1, 3, 640, 640]` float, outputs `dets [1, N, 5]` (x1, y1, x2, y2, score) and `labels [1, N]`. rtmlib then keeps `dets` with **score > 0.3** (a constant in its code for this kind of file: the constructor's `score_thr = 0.7` is not used here), divides the boxes by the letterbox ratio, and **ignores `labels`**. So the shipping operating point is "NMS of the graph, then score above 0.3, every class".
- Pre-processing: resize keeping the ratio, pad with 114, BGR, values 0 to 255, no normalisation.
- Unknown until measured: the NMS and score limits baked into the graph, and whether the model can output other classes than person (the `labels` output).

**The three questions and how they are measured.**

1. **Recall of the performer.**
   - *Set R:* the frames the maintainer and the agent labelled `ok` in P3.08 have a skeleton known to be right; its hull (box of the joints with a score of 0.3 or more, grown by 5 %) is the **reference box** of the performer; there are 170 such frames with a person and 2 without one (a title card and a black frame: they test false positives). A detector **finds** the performer when one of its boxes has IoU of 0.5 or more with the reference. Both detectors see the same frames, so the comparison is **paired** (exact McNemar test on the discordant frames). The frames come from the pool of P3.08, which only held frames where YOLOX and the tracker had succeeded, so recall on R **flatters YOLOX**; that bias is written in the report, and set C is the answer to it.
   - *Set C:* both detectors on every frame of the five clips (about 3 800 frames). For each frame: both find a person, only one, or neither. **The frames where only one detects are looked at** (at most 60, a seeded sample if there are more) and labelled by what is in the picture: a person (the performer) or not; that gives each detector's misses and false alarms on frames the pool of P3.08 did not choose. Frames where **both** miss cannot be seen by this method (stated).
2. **Stability of the box.** On set C, for the performer's box (the largest box of the frame with a height of at least 20 % of the image; the performer is one large person on these clips): the distribution of `1 − IoU` between consecutive frames (median, p95, p99), the number of frames with IoU under 0.5 (jumps), the relative change of the height against the median of its five neighbours, and **dropouts**: runs of frames **in real time** (not in frame counts) where the other detector still finds the performer but this one does not (a run longer than 0.5 s is a loss for the tracker: `max_lost_s` of P3.06).
3. **Speed and memory.** Set S, one fresh process per model (ONNX Runtime keeps its memory arena), CUDA provider, batch 1, 640 x 640, fp32, 30 warm-up and 300 measured frames, three repeats: model-only time (`session.run`) and end-to-end time (pre-process, run, post-process) as median, p95, p99; the VRAM above the baseline with the NVML sampler of P3.07. The share of the detector in the pipeline is read from the P3.07 numbers, to say what a faster detector would change (at N = 5 the detector runs on one frame in five).

**The rule, written before any measurement (to be confirmed, Q9).** Same method as P3.07: the rule is fixed first and applied as written.

- YOLO11 is **materially better** if **either** (R) its recall on set R is higher than YOLOX's by **3 percentage points or more** at the matched score limit of 0.3 **and** the exact McNemar two-sided p is **under 0.05**; **or** (C) in the reviewed frames of set C YOLO11 is right and YOLOX wrong in **at least 5 more frames** than the reverse. Both at a matched score limit of 0.3; the NMS IoU of YOLO11 is set to the 0.45 that YOLOX uses (its default of 0.7 is reported as a side line).
- Stability is **reported and not a criterion by itself**: between detector calls the box comes from the keypoints (N = 5), so the detector's jitter reaches the pose only on one frame in five. It becomes a reason if YOLO11's p95 of `1 − IoU` is **at most 0.7 times** YOLOX's **and** its recall is not lower.
- Speed and memory are **reported and not a criterion**: the detector is a small part of the time of the pipeline. *(Checked after the measurement against the P3.07 numbers: the premise was wrong; the detector is 73 % of the time at N = 1 and 44 % at N = 5. The rule did not depend on it, and the appendix says so.)*
- **Outcome:** not materially better → **DEC: DEC-0005 stands** (YOLOX stays, no Ultralytics, nothing else to do). Materially better → **do not adopt it** (AGPL): record the gap with its numbers, tell the maintainer, and open a follow-up to measure **permissive** detectors on the same sets; any use of an Ultralytics licence is a commercial decision for the maintainer.
- If a criterion cannot be evaluated (fewer than 20 positive frames in R, no discordant frames in C), the outcome is "no decision from this measurement" and says why; it is never silently "no gap".

**Unknowns, risks:**

1. **The reference boxes come from skeletons**, not from drawn boxes: a hull is a little tighter than a person box; IoU 0.5 and a 5 % margin are chosen so that a good detection passes. Written in the report; the rule uses the *difference* between detectors, which this affects equally.
2. **Selection bias of set R** (it flatters YOLOX) and **blindness to joint misses** (both miss): the report says so; set C reduces the first and cannot remove the second.
3. **Fairness of the two pipelines:** different pre-processing (YOLO11: RGB, scaled to 0 to 1, letterbox 640; YOLOX: BGR 0 to 255) and NMS inside (YOLOX) against NMS in our NumPy code (YOLO11). Speed is therefore given **model-only and end-to-end**; the thresholds are matched; the sensitivity to the thresholds is shown.
4. **Domain:** YOLOX here was trained on HumanArt (real and artistic people), YOLO11 on COCO; lesson footage of one large performer is neither. The result is for this footage.
5. **Safety of the export** (a pickle from a vendor, a large package with automatic behaviour): isolated environment, approval, one run, telemetry and installs off, the environment deleted at the close.
6. **Disk and time:** a CPU build of PyTorch is enough for the export (about 1 GB for the environment), the GPU is not needed there; the measurements need the GPU for a few minutes. The throwaway ingest of the videos (about 1.3 GB, as in P3.08) is done again and removed at the close.
7. **The version of Ultralytics decides the ONNX layout** (`[1, 84, 8400]` for a detector with 80 classes, boxes as centre and size): the adapter checks the shape and the class count and refuses anything else, naming what it found.
8. Two agents work in this tree: `bench/` is shared with P3.07; new files only, no change to its modules except where the plan says.

## 2. Plan

**Design.**

`taiji_ai/src/taiji_ai/bench/yolo_onnx.py` (new; numpy, OpenCV, ONNX Runtime; no Ultralytics):

- `letterbox(image_bgr, size) -> (padded_rgb_float, ratio, (pad_x, pad_y))`: the resize and the pad as the YOLO11 export expects (ratio kept, pad value 114, centred), the inverse mapping of a box.
- `decode_yolo(output, ratio, pad, conf, iou, person_class=0) -> (n, 5)`: reads `[1, 4 + C, A]`, takes the person score, filters, NMS in NumPy, maps back to image pixels; refuses another layout and names the shape and the class count it found.
- `YoloOnnx(path, device)`: the ONNX Runtime session through `pose.rtm.prepare_onnxruntime`; `detect(frame_bgr) -> (n, 5)`; model-only and end-to-end timing hooks.
- `ShippingDetector`: the registry's YOLOX file through the same ONNX Runtime session, **with the scores kept** (the rtmlib call drops them): same pre-processing as rtmlib, the graph's own NMS, the 0.3 limit applied by us so that other limits can be shown; a test checks that at 0.3 its boxes equal those of `PoseEstimator.detect` on real frames.

`taiji_ai/src/taiji_ai/bench/detector_metrics.py` (new, pure numpy, no I/O):

- `box_iou`, `reference_box(keypoints, scores, min_score=0.3, margin=0.05)`, `finds(detections, reference, iou_min=0.5)`;
- `recall_paired(found_a, found_b)`: counts of both, only A, only B, neither; `mcnemar_exact(b, c)`;
- `performer_box(detections, image_height, min_height_frac=0.2)`: the largest box of a frame;
- `stability(boxes_by_frame, t_sec)`: `1 − IoU` percentiles, jumps, height change, **dropout runs by real time** (not frames);
- `agreement(found_a, found_b)` for set C;
- `DetectorRule` and `decide(...)`: the rule of section 1 as a function, with its boundaries, naming the criterion that decided and the cases that cannot be evaluated.

`taiji_ai/src/taiji_ai/bench/detector_worker.py` (new): the subprocess worker of one model: loads the model, runs set S (timing, NVML), runs sets R and C, writes `detections_<model>.npz` and a JSON; same pattern as `bench/worker.py` (fresh process per model).

`taiji_ai/tools/detector_compare.py` (new): subcommands `run` (finds the D20 videos by size and SHA-256, throwaway ingest, decodes the frames, one worker per model, metrics, `detector.json`), `sheets` (the discordant frames, tiles with both boxes, a review page like P3.08), `report` (tables from `detector.json` and the reviewed labels); labels only in the output, exit code 2 for what it cannot do.

`taiji_ai/tools/` and the notes for the isolated export (a short, committed `Docs/tasks/P3.09.md` section and a scratch script **outside** the repository): the commands for creating the environment, installing the pinned package, downloading the file, computing the SHA-256 and calling `yolo export format=onnx imgsz=640 opset=17 simplify=False dynamic=False`.

**Refinements made when the rule became a function, 2026-11-17, before any number was measured** (DEC-0043 carries them):

1. The unit of criterion C is a **run** (a stretch of consecutive frames in which exactly one detector has a performer-sized box),
   with one representative frame (the middle one) per run, at most 60 runs: counting every frame of a long run would count one
   event many times. "At least 5 more frames" therefore reads "at least 5 more runs".
2. **Stability never decides alone**: it is listed as a supporting reason (p95 of `1 - IoU` at most 0.7 times YOLOX's and no
   recall loss) or as a note; this removes the ambiguity of "a reason only together with that".
3. The score limit is **strict** for both detectors (a score equal to the limit is dropped), as rtmlib does for YOLOX.
4. **Found after the first run (2026-11-17):** the NMS limit inside the shipping graph is **0.65** (read from the ONNX file with the
   `onnx` package of the isolated environment: max 200 boxes per class, score limit 0.01), not the 0.45 written in the plan; the final run gives YOLO11 0.65
   (the first run, 0.45, had the same recall, and 0.7 as a side line the same again). The first run also showed that the labelled frames
   need a second view of the boxes, apart from the rule: YOLOX's boxes are **looser** than the confirmed skeleton's hull, so
   at IoU 0.5 it loses three frames that it finds at lower limits; the tool now also reports the best IoU per frame and the recall at
   IoU 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7 (the rule still uses 0.5).
5. "A detector has the performer" in set C means a box with a height of at least 20 % of the image height and a score above
   the limit; in set R it means a box with IoU of 0.5 or more with the reference box.

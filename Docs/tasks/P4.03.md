## 1. Analysis

**Goal:** the third step of the quality layer (W6). Pose keypoints sometimes jump for a frame or two (the `flicker` of P3.08)
and then come back. The Hampel filter compares each observed value of a joint with the median of its neighbours in a short
window; a value that is further from that median than `n_sigma` robust standard deviations (`1.4826 x MAD`) is an **outlier**:
it is flagged, set to NaN and given the reason `outlier`. Nothing is smoothed or corrected here (AGENTS.md 5.1: no fabricated
observations); the hole that an outlier leaves is the business of P4.02, which bridges it if it is short, and flags the bridge.
The raw and mask layers that are read are never changed (5.3).

**What is not obvious, and why this plan has a measurement in it.** The rule of W6 (window 7, 3 sigma from the MAD) is a
small-sample rule: the MAD of seven numbers is a noisy estimate. A simulation on **pure Gaussian noise** (200,000 samples,
no spikes at all) flags **4.8 %** of the samples per coordinate at window 7 and 3 sigma, and **10.8 %** if "3 x MAD" is read
without the 1.4826; 3.4 % at 3.5 sigma; 2.5 % at 4 sigma; at window 11 and 3 sigma 2.9 %. A cell is flagged if either of its
two coordinates is, so about twice that per cell. On real footage whose joints jitter by a pixel or two, the rule as written
could remove a tenth of the good cells in still stretches. So this task implements the rule with its numbers as **parameters**,
measures what it does on the D20 footage and on the 250 frames that a person labelled in P3.08 (how many good cells it flags,
how many of the named wrong joints it finds), and **the maintainer chooses the defaults with those numbers in front of them**.

**The rule for one coordinate of one joint (x and y are done apart; a cell is an outlier if either is):**

1. The window of a cell is the `window` rows centred on it (the rows at the edges of the series are simply absent). A row of
   the window counts only if its cell is **observed** (`reason == ok`) and its time is within `max_half_span_s` of the
   centre's time (so a stall or a long loss in the middle of a window cuts it; real seconds, not a frame rate).
2. The cell is **judged** only if it is observed itself and at least `min_observed` cells of the window (itself included)
   count. A cell that is not judged is never flagged.
3. `m` = the median of the counted values, `MAD` = the median of `|value - m|` over them, `sigma = max(1.4826 x MAD,
   scale_floor_px)`.
4. The cell is an outlier if `|value - m| > n_sigma x sigma` (strictly: equal is kept). With `MAD = 0` and no floor any
   deviation counts, and an identical value does not: a spike in a window that is otherwise constant is found, and a still
   joint is not touched.

The rule needs no frame rate: the window is a number of rows, the time only cuts it at a stall. Spikes are judged against
the **original** values of the window (one pass, no iteration, not against values that other flags removed).

**Unknowns, risks, questions** (the maintainer is asked; the proposed answer is applied if there is no other):

- **Q1. The window: rows or seconds?** W6 says "window 7". **Proposed: 7 rows**, with real time used only to cut the window at
  a stall (`max_half_span_s` 0.25 s: 2.5 times the 3 rows of a nominal 30 fps). A window of seconds would give 5 rows at 24 fps
  and 13 at 60 fps, and the MAD of five numbers is worse than the MAD of seven; a window of rows lets the estimate keep its
  quality at every rate and spans 125 ms at 24 fps, 100 ms at 30 fps, 50 ms at 60 fps. The alternative costs some statistical
  quality at low rates for a rate-independent physical window.
- **Q2. "3 sigma (MAD)" or "3 x MAD"?** The plan row says `3·MAD`, W6 says `3σ (MAD)`. **Proposed: sigma = 1.4826 x MAD** (the
  usual Hampel; the number that makes the MAD a standard deviation for Gaussian noise). Without it the rule is a 2.0 sigma rule
  and flags 10.8 % of pure noise instead of 4.8 %.
- **Q3. x and y apart, or the distance?** **Proposed: apart, a cell is an outlier if either coordinate is** (the classic form, easy
  to test). A drawback: a joint that moves only vertically has almost no spread in x, so a small jitter in x can pass 3 sigma; the
  scale floor of Q5 is the remedy. The alternative (the distance to the 2D median) is rotation invariant and has one scale, but
  its distribution is not Gaussian and the rule of thumb loses its meaning.
- **Q4. What does an outlier become, and in which order do the stages run?** **Proposed:** NaN with the new reason code
  `OUTLIER = 5`, added to the registry of `quality/mask.py` (one registry for P4.07's coverage by reason; the P4.01 test of the
  names changes by one entry). Order: **mask → Hampel → gaps → resample → filter**, so that a one-frame spike is removed and its
  hole is bridged and flagged by P4.02 (`OUTLIER` is not in `never_fill`, so it is bridgeable), and Hampel never sees bridged
  values. P4.07 wires the order; the gap layer already works on any layer with NaN and a reason.
- **Q5. The defaults.** The numbers of M-QLT-03 will say how many good cells each setting flags and how many known wrong joints it
  finds. **Proposed: write the rule with the W6 values as the starting point, measure the settings listed below, show the table,
  and let the maintainer choose** `n_sigma` and `scale_floor_px` (and whether the variant with one noise scale per joint is worth
  adding) before the defaults are fixed in the code. Settings measured: (7, 3 sigma, no floor) as written; 3.5 and 4 sigma; floors of
  1, 2 and 3 px and the same as a share of the torso; and, as a measurement only, a variant that takes the scale from the whole
  track of the joint (the MAD of the residuals from the running median) instead of from seven samples. If the maintainer picks the
  variant it is built in this task (about 0.25 day more).
- **Risk: it removes good data.** See the simulation above; the table of M-QLT-03 is the answer, and every cell removed is NaN
  with a reason and may be bridged and flagged, never silently changed.
- **Risk: it cannot see what matters most.** A persistent wrong joint (`joint_off` over many frames) and a left/right swap are
  seen by every sample of the window, so the median follows them. P3.08 labelled 4 flickers in 61 failures; the sensitivity
  numbers will be small and biased (the review took frames that the rules ranked as suspect, among them a joint-jump rule). They
  say whether Hampel is worth its cost, not how often the pose fails.
- **Risk: a fast real movement is flagged.** Wrists and elbows move fastest; the measurement reports the share flagged by joint
  group, and the test of smooth large movements (SYN-05) is part of the acceptance.
- **Risk: cost.** A window of 7 on `T x 26 x 2` values is a large array for a long video; the work is done in blocks of rows with an
  overlap, and a test compares blocks with one pass.

## 2. Plan

**Design:**

```python
# quality/filters.py
MAD_TO_SIGMA: Final = 1.4826          # the MAD of Gaussian noise times this is its standard deviation

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class HampelParams:
    window: int = 7                     # odd, >= 3: rows centred on the cell
    n_sigma: float = 3.0                # > 0, finite
    min_observed: int = 5               # 3 <= min_observed <= window
    max_half_span_s: float = 0.25       # > 0: rows farther than this in real time are left out of the window
    scale_floor_px: float = 0.0         # >= 0: sigma is never smaller than this

def hampel_outliers(keypoints, reason, t_sec, params=None) -> HampelResult: ...
def hampel_track(masked: MaskedTrack, params=None) -> HampelResult: ...
```

Data flow: validate (the same contract as `interpolate_gaps`: shapes, dtypes, `t_sec` strictly increasing, NaN exactly where
`reason != ok`) → in blocks of rows (with `window // 2` rows of overlap): build the windows of each coordinate with
`sliding_window_view` on NaN-padded arrays, set to NaN the rows of a window that are not observed or are too far in time → median,
MAD and the count with `nanmedian` → the judged and outlier masks → write the result arrays. `hampel_track` passes
`masked.mask.keypoints`, `masked.mask.reason`, `masked.t_sec`. The reason code is added to `mask.py`: `OUTLIER: Final = 5` and
`"outlier"` at the end of `REASON_NAMES`.

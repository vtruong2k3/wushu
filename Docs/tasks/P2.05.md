## 1. Analysis

**Goal:** check the audio offset with an independent signal. The capture protocol (§5) switches a light on with each clap,
visible to both cameras; the frame where the light first appears in A and in B, mapped through the audio sync, must agree. The
difference is the residual that decides whether the two cameras may be combined in metrics (P2.06).

**Facts read (2026-11-15):** W2 step 4 (validate by a visible marker, residual in ms and frames); T-SYNC-04 (flash at a known
frame: found at that frame); the protocol locks exposure (§4), so a light causes a step in the mean brightness; `iter_frames`
decodes a time window with real timestamps.

**In scope:** `io/flash.py`: `brightness_series(path, t0, t1, rotation, downscale) -> (t_sec, luma)` (mean luma per frame on a
downscaled image), `find_flash_onsets(t, luma) -> list[float]` (rising steps), `validate_sync(audio_sync, rec_a, rec_b) ->
SyncValidation` (pairs the onsets of A and B in the start and end windows, residual = `t_b − a_to_b(t_a)`, in ms and in B's
frames, the larger of start and end).

**Out of scope:** the usability rule (P2.06), any other marker (a clapperboard, an LED pattern).

**Design:** a rising step is a frame-to-frame increase of the mean luma larger than a multiple of the robust spread (median
absolute deviation) of the differences in the window and larger than an absolute minimum; the onset time is the timestamp of
the first bright frame. Both minimums are engineering parameters set on the fixtures and recorded in `sync.json`. Onsets are
paired by order within the window (two flashes per signal); a pair count mismatch is reported, not forced. A residual is
quantised to the frame time, so the result states both ms and frames.
